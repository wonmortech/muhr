01 · What it proves

It proves when. Not who.

We state the limit up front: the seal shows the date and the integrity. Who signed is shown by the e-signature itself.

Proves

  • It existed by this dateBy Bitcoin block time, accurate to a few hours.
  • Not a single byte changedIf the hash differs, so does the file.
  • Checkable without usThe receipt, the open ots client and Bitcoin headers are enough.

One letter, a completely different hash. That is why a hash is a file’s fingerprint.

Does not prove

  • Who wrote or signed itAn EIMZO (PKCS#7) signature does that. Muhr only displays it.
  • That the content is trueMuhr does not read or judge the file.
  • NotarisationIt is extra, independently checkable evidence. It does not replace an official RFC 3161 timestamp.
02 · How it works

Five steps. Your file stays on your device.

Only a 64-character hash is sent. Everything else is open standards.

  1. Hash

    SHA-256 is computed in the browser or the CLI.

    Works below

  2. Log

    The hash goes into an open, append-only Merkle log (RFC 9162 style).

    Soon

  3. Bitcoin

    The log root reaches a Bitcoin block through public OpenTimestamps calendars. It takes a few hours.

    Soon

  4. Receipt

    .muhr.json: an inclusion proof plus the .ots file. You keep it.

    Soon

  5. Verify

    Browser, CLI or Telegram bot. Works even if our server is gone.

    Soon

Even if Wonmor disappears, the proof still works: verification relies on Bitcoin headers and the open ots client.
